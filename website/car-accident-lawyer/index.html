Right After a Crash

What to Do After a Thousand Oaks Car Accident

If you are reading this later, some of these will not apply — do what you still can.

Get Checked by a Doctor

Adrenaline can mask injuries for a day or more. Prompt medical care protects your health and creates a record close in time to the crash, which matters if the injury is later questioned.

Report and Document

Call the police if there are injuries or significant damage, and note the traffic collision report number. Photograph the vehicles, their positions, the roadway, and any visible injuries.

Exchange and Collect Information

Get the other driver’s name, license, insurance, and plate number, along with the names and numbers of any witnesses before they leave.

Be Cautious With the Other Insurer

You can decline a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurance company until you have spoken with a lawyer. Avoid accepting a fast settlement before you know how the injury will develop.

Common Collisions

Types and Causes of Crashes We See

Most car accident claims fall into a handful of patterns.

Rear-End Collisions

Common in stop-and-go traffic and at busy signals. The following driver is often, though not always, primarily at fault.

Intersection and Left-Turn Crashes

Disputes frequently come down to who had the green light and whether a turning driver had a clear gap.

Freeway and Lane-Change Crashes

Merging traffic and sudden lane changes at freeway speed, where several vehicles are often involved.

Distracted Driving

Phone use, navigation screens, and general inattention, which often surface later through phone records or an admission.

Impaired or Reckless Driving

Speeding, street racing, and driving under the influence, which can affect both liability and the categories of damages available.

Single-Vehicle and Roadway Conditions

Crashes involving road design, an obstruction, or a poorly maintained surface, where responsibility may extend beyond the drivers involved.

The Insurance Company

Dealing With the Insurance Adjuster

After a crash, you will usually hear from one or more adjusters quickly. It helps to know what to expect.

The other driver’s insurer is not on your side, even when the representative is friendly. Common steps include asking for a recorded statement, requesting broad access to your medical history, and making an early settlement offer before treatment is finished. Each of these can work against you.

Your own insurer is a different relationship — your policy generally requires cooperation — but it is still an insurance company evaluating a claim. Whether you are dealing with the other driver’s carrier, your own uninsured-motorist coverage, or a rideshare policy, it is reasonable to have a lawyer handle those communications so the claim is not narrowed by an offhand comment or a rushed decision.

California Law

How Fault Works in a California Car Accident

California is an at-fault state. The driver who caused the crash, and that driver’s insurance, is generally responsible for the resulting harm. Sorting out who was at fault is often where a claim is won or lost.

California also follows a pure comparative fault rule. If more than one person shares the blame, each is responsible for their percentage. If you are found 20 percent at fault, for example, your recovery is generally reduced by 20 percent, but it is not eliminated, and even a driver who is mostly at fault can recover something. Insurers know this and often argue for a larger share of fault on the injured person to reduce what they pay, which is one reason the police report’s initial view of fault is not the final word.

Compensation

Categories of Damages in a Car Accident Claim

What a claim may include depends on the injuries, the treatment, the available coverage, and the assessment of fault. In general terms, California recognizes:

  • Medical expenses, past and future
  • Lost income, and reduced earning capacity in serious cases
  • Vehicle repair or replacement and related out-of-pocket costs
  • Physical pain, emotional distress, and loss of enjoyment of normal activities
  • Permanent scarring, disfigurement, or disability

In a smaller number of cases — for example, a crash caused by drunk driving — additional categories of damages may be available. If a crash results in a death, California wrongful death law provides for a separate set of damages recoverable by surviving family members. No specific outcome can be promised; the value of a claim depends entirely on its facts.

Uninsured and Underinsured Drivers

When the Other Driver Has No Insurance or Too Little

Not every driver on the road carries insurance, and many who do carry only the state minimum, which can fall well short of the cost of a serious injury.

If you have uninsured motorist (UM) or underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage on your own auto policy, it can step in when the at-fault driver cannot cover your losses, including, under many policies, in a hit-and-run. This coverage comes with its own rules: there are notice requirements, and there is generally a separate deadline to formally demand arbitration, apart from the deadline to sue the other driver. Because these time limits are easy to miss, it is worth having your policy reviewed early even if you are not sure the coverage applies.

Time Limits

Deadlines to Keep in Mind

California sets deadlines for bringing a car accident claim, and they are firm. In general:

The deadline to file a lawsuit for personal injury is usually two years from the date of the crash. The deadline for vehicle and property damage is usually three years.

Shorter deadlines apply in specific situations. If a government vehicle or a dangerous public road is involved, a formal written claim to the government entity is usually required within six months. When the injured person is a minor, the timeline can work differently. Uninsured and underinsured motorist claims carry their own separate deadlines under the policy.

These are general rules, and exceptions exist in both directions. Because a missed deadline can end an otherwise valid claim, the safest approach is to confirm the specific deadline that applies to your situation as early as possible.

Building the Claim

Evidence That Can Matter in a Car Accident Case

Some of the most useful evidence is only available for a limited time, which is another reason not to wait.

  • The traffic collision report and any officer’s observations
  • Photographs of the vehicles, the scene, and the injuries
  • Names and statements from witnesses
  • Medical records and bills documenting the injury and treatment
  • Vehicle damage, repair estimates, and in some cases event data recorder (“black box”) information
  • Video from nearby businesses, doorbell cameras, or a dashcam
  • Records showing time missed from work and its financial effect
Representation

When Hiring a Car Accident Lawyer May Be Worthwhile

Not every fender-bender needs a lawyer. It is more likely to be worth talking to one when:

  • You were injured and needed more than a single visit to a doctor
  • The other driver or their insurer disputes who was at fault
  • More than one vehicle, or more than one insurance company, is involved
  • The other driver was uninsured, underinsured, or fled the scene
  • A commercial vehicle, rideshare driver, or government vehicle was involved
  • The settlement offer does not seem to account for your future treatment or lost income

Frequently Asked Questions

Thousand Oaks Car Accident Questions

The other driver's insurance company called me. Do I have to talk to them?
You are generally not required to give the other driver's insurer a recorded statement, and you can decline until you have spoken with a lawyer. You may have a duty to cooperate with your own insurer under your policy, which is a separate matter.
How long do I have to file a car accident claim in California?
For injuries, the deadline to file a lawsuit is generally two years from the date of the crash; for vehicle and property damage it is generally three years. Shorter deadlines apply if a government entity is involved, often a written claim within six months, and uninsured motorist claims have their own separate deadlines. Exceptions exist, so it is best to confirm the deadline that applies to your situation early.
The crash was partly my fault. Can I still recover anything?
Possibly. California uses a pure comparative fault rule, so being partly at fault generally reduces your recovery by your share of the blame rather than barring it. Fault is often disputed, and the insurer's first assessment is not final.
The other driver had no insurance. What now?
If you carry uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage, it may apply, including in many hit-and-run situations. That coverage has its own notice requirements and deadlines, so it is worth having your policy reviewed rather than assuming it does not help.
Should I accept the insurance company's first offer?
Not without understanding what it leaves out. Early offers are often made before the full cost of medical treatment and lost income is known. Once a claim is settled, it generally cannot be reopened if the injury turns out to be worse than expected.
What does it cost to hire the firm for a car accident case?
The consultation is free. Car accident matters are handled on a contingency fee: attorney's fees are a percentage of any recovery, set out in a written agreement, and if there is no recovery you owe no attorney's fee. Case costs are addressed separately in the agreement.
Do I have to go to court for a car accident claim?
Often not. Many car accident claims settle through negotiation. Some require filing a lawsuit, and of those, most still resolve before trial. Preparing the case thoroughly is part of what makes a fair settlement possible.
