Car Accident Claims

After a car accident, the legal claim is only one part of the problem.

There may be medical treatment, missed work, a damaged vehicle, rental-car issues, multiple insurance carriers, and questions about who was at fault. The firm organizes the legal side of the claim so that evidence, coverage issues, and damages can be evaluated in a structured way.

Alexey Savostyanov communicates with clients directly, reviews the available evidence, deals with insurers, and explains the choices that matter as the claim develops.

Right After a Crash

Four practical priorities after a collision.

Get appropriate medical care.

Prompt evaluation protects your health and creates a contemporaneous record if symptoms later become disputed.

Document what you can.

Preserve photographs, vehicle positions, roadway conditions, visible injuries, insurance information, and the collision-report number if one exists.

Keep witness and insurance details.

Names, telephone numbers, policy information, plate numbers, and available witness details can become important later.

Be careful with early statements and releases.

Recorded statements and quick settlement paperwork can affect a claim before the medical course or full damages are known.

Insurance Strategy

The insurer's first position is not the final word.

Liability, causation, medical treatment, policy limits, comparative fault, and the value of claimed losses can all become points of dispute. A careful claim is built from evidence rather than from assumptions about what an adjuster will accept.

Common Collision Patterns

Different crashes create different evidence questions.

Rear-End Collisions

Often associated with stop-and-go traffic, freeway congestion, and signals. The circumstances still matter when fault is disputed.

Intersection and Left-Turn Crashes

Signal phase, right of way, sight lines, vehicle positions, and witness accounts can become central.

Freeway and Lane-Change Crashes

Speed, merge behavior, lane position, and multiple-vehicle involvement can complicate liability analysis.

Distracted Driving

Phone use, inattention, navigation, and delayed reaction can become relevant to how the collision occurred.

Impaired or Reckless Driving

Speed, unsafe maneuvers, intoxication evidence, and law-enforcement records may materially affect the case.

Roadway or Third-Party Issues

Some crashes raise questions about a commercial vehicle, road condition, obstruction, or another responsible party beyond the drivers.

Car Accident FAQ

Questions that often come up early in a claim.

The other driver's insurance company called me. Do I have to talk to them?

You are generally not required to give the other driver's insurer a recorded statement before getting legal advice. Your own insurer may have separate cooperation requirements under your policy.

How long do I have to file a car accident claim in California?

Many injury claims are subject to a two-year limitations period, but other deadlines can apply to property damage, government claims, uninsured motorist matters, and special circumstances. Confirm the applicable deadline early.

The crash was partly my fault. Can I still recover anything?

California follows comparative fault principles. Partial responsibility can reduce a recovery rather than necessarily eliminate it.

The other driver had no insurance. What now?

Uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage may apply. Policy terms, notice requirements, and deadlines should be reviewed promptly.

Should I accept the insurance company's first offer?

Before signing a release, compare the offer against the available evidence, medical course, lost income, property damage, and other potentially recoverable losses.

What does it cost to hire the firm for a car accident case?

The consultation is free. Car accident matters are handled on a contingency fee set out in a written agreement. Case costs are addressed separately in that agreement.

Do I have to go to court for a car accident claim?

Often not. Many claims resolve through negotiation, while some require litigation when liability or value remains disputed. Filing suit does not necessarily mean the case will be tried.
