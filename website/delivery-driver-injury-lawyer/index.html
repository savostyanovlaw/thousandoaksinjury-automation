When a delivery driver injures someone else

A pedestrian, cyclist, motorist, or resident may be injured by a delivery van, truck, motorcycle, bicycle, or other vehicle. The claim may involve the driver's negligence, the driver's employer or contracting entity where legally appropriate, and available insurance. A company logo on a vehicle does not by itself establish which company is legally responsible.

Preserve vehicle and license-plate information, the driver's name, delivery-service details, witness contacts, and any camera footage. See our car accident and rideshare and car-sharing resources for related issues.

When the delivery worker is injured

Couriers delivering for Amazon, DoorDash, Uber Eats, Instacart, FedEx, UPS, or a local business may be injured in a road collision, while approaching a home, or on commercial property. Depending on employment status and the facts, workers' compensation, a third-party personal injury claim, or both may need evaluation. Classification and available benefits should not be assumed from an app or uniform.

Dog bites during deliveries

A courier may be bitten or knocked down by a dog while making a delivery to a residence or business. Photograph the injury and location, identify the dog and owner when safe, obtain medical care, and preserve delivery records. California dog-bite liability depends on the applicable law and facts; a separate fall or other injury may raise additional issues. Learn more about dog bite injury claims.

Assaults, robberies, and negligent security

Delivery workers can be assaulted, threatened, or robbed while picking up or dropping off an order. The attacker may be responsible; in some circumstances a property owner, business, or other party may also face civil liability if there is a legally sufficient basis, such as foreseeable danger and a failure to take reasonable security measures. A crime on the premises does not automatically establish property-owner liability.

When safe, contact emergency services or law enforcement, seek treatment, and preserve incident reports, platform messages, surveillance requests, and witness details.

Slips, falls, stairs, and unsafe entrances

Broken steps, poor lighting, slippery walkways, and other dangerous conditions can injure delivery personnel. Liability may depend on control of the property, notice of the hazard, and the circumstances of entry. Review our slip-and-fall injury resource. If an employer or insurer is involved, preserve the claim and benefit paperwork as well.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an Amazon or DoorDash delivery worker bring a personal injury claim?

Potentially. A claim against a negligent third party may be available depending on the facts. Workers' compensation and employment classification may also affect the available remedies.

Can I make a claim if a delivery driver hit my car or injured me?

Possibly. Liability depends on the collision evidence, the driver's conduct, any relationship to a delivery business, and available insurance.

What if a dog bites me while delivering an order?

Seek medical attention and preserve evidence. California dog-bite law and any available employment benefits or third-party claims should be assessed based on the specific circumstances.

Can a delivery platform be liable for an assault?

Not simply because an assault happened during a delivery. A potential claim against a platform, business, or property owner requires a separate factual and legal basis.
