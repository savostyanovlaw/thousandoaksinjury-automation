Personal Injury Representation

Help After a Serious Injury

Personal injury law covers situations where someone is hurt because another person or company was careless — a driver who ran a light, a property owner who ignored a hazard, a commercial vehicle operated without care. If that has happened to you, the practical questions are usually the same: who pays for the medical care, what happens with the time missed at work, and whether the insurance company’s first offer is actually fair.

Savostyanov Law Corporation handles these matters from the first conversation through resolution. Alexey Savostyanov meets with you directly and explains how your claim is likely to be evaluated. Consultations are free, and injury matters are handled on a contingency fee: attorney’s fees are a percentage of any recovery, set out in a written agreement before work begins, and if there is no recovery you owe no attorney’s fee.

Right After an Accident

Steps That Usually Help After an Injury

Every situation is different, but these steps tend to protect both your health and any claim you may have.

Get Medical Attention

See a doctor promptly, even if you feel only sore. Some injuries are not obvious at first, and a gap in treatment is one of the first things an insurer points to.

Document What You Can

Photograph vehicles, the scene, and visible injuries. Get the other driver’s insurance information and names for any witnesses. Keep the traffic collision report number if officers respond.

Be Careful With Insurance Calls

You are generally not required to give the other driver’s insurer a recorded statement before speaking with a lawyer. Early statements and quick settlement offers can limit a claim before the full injury picture is known.

The Process

How a Personal Injury Claim Generally Works

No two claims follow the exact same path, but most move through recognizable stages.

It usually starts with a consultation and an investigation — gathering the collision report, photographs, witness information, and insurance details, and identifying who may be responsible.

While that happens, you focus on medical treatment. A claim is easier to evaluate once your treatment has stabilized and the lasting effects of the injury are clearer, so there is often a reason not to rush.

Once the picture is complete, the claim is usually presented to the insurance company with supporting records, followed by negotiation. Many claims resolve at this stage. If the insurer will not offer a fair amount, the next step is filing a lawsuit, which is followed by discovery — the formal exchange of evidence and testimony — and often mediation. A case can settle at any point along the way. Relatively few go all the way to trial, though some do, and the possibility of trial is part of what a claim is worth.

California also sets deadlines for filing suit. Those deadlines vary depending on who is involved and the type of claim, and missing one can end a case regardless of its merits, which is the main reason to talk to a lawyer early rather than late.

Compensation

What an Injury Claim Can Cover

What a claim may include depends on the facts — the injuries, the treatment, the available insurance, and how fault is assessed. In California, personal injury compensation is generally divided into two categories.

Economic damages are measurable financial losses, such as past and future medical treatment; lost wages and, in serious cases, reduced earning capacity; out-of-pocket costs connected to the injury; and vehicle repair or replacement.

Non-economic damages address the effects that do not come with a receipt, such as physical pain, emotional distress, loss of enjoyment of activities, and permanent scarring or disability.

California follows a comparative fault rule, so if you are found partly responsible for what happened, your recovery is generally reduced by your share of the fault rather than eliminated. The value of any particular claim depends on its own facts, and no result can be promised or predicted.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions About Personal Injury Claims

Do I need a lawyer after a minor accident?
Not always. If injuries are minor and liability is clear, some people handle a small claim on their own. It is more likely to be worth speaking with a lawyer when there are injuries that need ongoing treatment, when fault is disputed, when more than one vehicle or insurer is involved, or when an offer seems low. The consultation is free, so it costs nothing to have the situation looked at.
What does it cost to hire the firm?
The consultation is free. Personal injury matters are handled on a contingency fee, which means attorney's fees are a percentage of any recovery and are set out in a written fee agreement before work begins. If there is no recovery, you owe no attorney's fee. Case costs, such as records fees, filing fees, or expert fees, are separate from attorney's fees, and how those are handled is explained in the agreement.
How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in California?
For most personal injury claims, California generally allows two years from the date of the injury to file a lawsuit. Important exceptions exist. Claims against a government entity usually require a formal written claim within six months. Different deadlines can apply when the injured person is a minor, when an injury was not discovered right away, or in medical negligence cases. Because missing a deadline can bar a claim entirely, it is best to confirm the applicable time limit early rather than assume.
Should I give the insurance company a recorded statement?
You are generally not required to give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurance company before speaking with a lawyer, and it is reasonable to wait. Your own policy may require you to cooperate with your insurer, which is a separate question. In either case, what you say early, before you know the full extent of your injuries, can be used to reduce or dispute the claim later.
What if the other driver had no insurance, or not enough?
If you carry uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage on your own auto policy, it may provide compensation when the at-fault driver has no insurance or too little to cover your losses. There can be notice requirements and time limits tied to that coverage, so it is worth having your policy reviewed rather than assuming it does not apply.
Will my case have to go to court?
Many personal injury claims are resolved through negotiation with the insurance company without a lawsuit. Some require filing suit, either because liability is disputed or because the offer is not reasonable. Filing a lawsuit does not necessarily mean a trial, and most filed cases still resolve before trial, but preparing a case as though it may be tried is part of what makes a fair resolution possible.
What if I was partly at fault for the accident?
California uses a comparative fault rule. If you are found partly responsible, your recovery is generally reduced by your percentage of fault rather than eliminated. Fault is frequently disputed, and an insurer's initial assessment of who was at fault is not the last word.
How long does a personal injury case take?
It varies widely. A straightforward claim might resolve in a few months; a case involving serious injuries, disputed fault, or a lawsuit can take a year or more. One reason cases take time is that it is usually better to understand the full course of your medical treatment before settling, since a claim resolved too early can leave out costs that were not yet known.
