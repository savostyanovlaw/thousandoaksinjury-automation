Practical Guidance

Start with the questions that matter after an accident.

Protect your health first.

Seek appropriate medical evaluation when you are injured. Early records can also help document when symptoms began and how they developed.

Preserve information.

Keep photographs, insurance correspondence, medical records, repair information, witness details, and other materials related to the incident.

Be careful with recorded statements.

What you say before the full extent of an injury is understood can later become part of an insurance dispute. Your obligations can differ between your own insurer and another party's insurer.

Confirm deadlines early.

Different California claims can carry different limitations periods and notice rules, particularly when a government entity is involved.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions people often ask before hiring a personal injury lawyer.

Do I need a lawyer after a minor accident?

Not always. It becomes more important to get legal advice when injuries require ongoing treatment, fault is disputed, multiple insurers are involved, or an offer appears too low. A consultation with the firm is free.

What does it cost to hire the firm?

The consultation is free. Personal injury matters are handled on a contingency fee set out in a written agreement. Case costs are addressed separately in that agreement.

How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in California?

Many California personal injury claims have a two-year limitations period, but important exceptions apply. Government claims may require action within six months, and other circumstances can change the deadline. Confirm the applicable deadline early.

Should I give the insurance company a recorded statement?

You generally do not have to give the other driver's insurer a recorded statement before getting legal advice. Your own policy can impose separate cooperation duties.

What if the other driver had no insurance, or not enough?

Uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage may apply. The actual policy, notice requirements, and deadlines need to be reviewed.

Will my case have to go to court?

Many claims resolve through negotiation. Some require litigation when liability or value remains disputed. Filing a lawsuit does not necessarily mean a case will go to trial.

What if I was partly at fault?

California applies comparative fault principles. Partial responsibility can reduce a recovery without necessarily eliminating it.

How long does a personal injury case take?

Timing varies with the severity of injuries, medical treatment, liability disputes, insurance issues, and whether litigation becomes necessary.
