Establish what happened before debating blame

A claim should be evaluated from the evidence rather than assumptions about riders. The investigation can involve a left turn, merging vehicle, rear-end impact, unsafe lane change, road condition, or multiple vehicles. Photographs, witness accounts, camera recordings, the vehicles, and crash reports may help establish responsibility.

The firm is based in Westlake Village and handles personal injury matters throughout California. Tell the attorney the exact location, including whether the crash occurred on US-101, a local street, or another road. The location and the responsible parties affect the investigation and potential claim.

Preserve evidence specific to the motorcycle

The bike and gear

Photograph the motorcycle, helmet, clothing, and damage before repair or disposal. Keep towing, storage, inspection, and repair records. Discuss preserving damaged items with the attorney.

Video and witnesses

Keep original helmet-camera or dashcam files and witness contacts. Identify nearby cameras promptly. A request to preserve footage should be directed to the person or business controlling it.

Medical and work records

Keep treatment records, bills, and documentation of missed work. Describe your symptoms accurately to your providers and follow their medical recommendations.

Lane splitting and insurance need a fact-specific review

The California DMV explains that lane sharing with motorcycles is legal. That does not establish who is at fault in a particular collision. Speed, visibility, traffic, vehicle movements, and each participant’s conduct still matter. Preserve evidence showing how the collision occurred.

Potential insurance sources can include the other driver’s liability coverage and applicable uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage. Availability depends on the policies and facts. Save your policy documents and insurer communications; do not assume that an offered amount resolves every part of your claim.

Motorcycle accident questions

Does lane splitting automatically prevent a claim?

No. Lane sharing is legal in California, but liability in a particular crash depends on the circumstances and evidence.

What if the other driver has little or no insurance?

The attorney can review available coverage, including any applicable uninsured or underinsured motorist policy. Coverage and recovery are not guaranteed.

What does representation cost?

The consultation is free. Personal injury representation is governed by a written contingency fee agreement addressing fees and case costs separately.

Ask about deadlines promptly

California’s general deadline for an injury or wrongful-death lawsuit is two years under Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1. Different rules and exceptions can apply, including to medical negligence and claims involving children. Claims against public entities generally require an earlier claim presentation, often within six months under Government Code § 911.2. Do not assume that negotiations with an insurer extend a deadline. A consultation should address the particular dates and defendants in your case.
