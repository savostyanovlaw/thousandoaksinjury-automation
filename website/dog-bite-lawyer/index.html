California dog bite law: what must be established?

Civil Code § 3342 generally holds a dog’s owner responsible for injuries caused by a bite occurring in a public place or while the injured person is lawfully on private property. A prior bite or the owner’s knowledge of dangerous behavior is not required for this statutory claim. Ownership, the bite, lawful presence, injury, and causation still need to be established. Defenses and exceptions may apply; this is not a guarantee of recovery.

An injury caused by being knocked down or chased without a bite requires a different analysis. A keeper, landlord, or other person is not automatically liable under the owner’s dog bite statute. Negligence, control, prior knowledge, and the particular circumstances may matter. See California jury instructions 462–463.

What to preserve after a dog attack

The dog and its owner

Keep the owner’s contact information, photographs identifying the dog, the location, witness contacts, and any animal-control report number. Preserve communications about the incident and any known earlier attacks.

The injury and treatment

Seek appropriate medical care. Keep treatment records, bills, photographs documenting the wounds and healing, and evidence of missed work. Do not discard damaged clothing or other relevant items.

Insurance and other evidence

Homeowners, renters, or other liability insurance may be relevant, depending on the policy and circumstances. Save insurance communications and identify nearby cameras before footage is overwritten.

Dog bite questions

Does the dog need a history of biting?

A prior bite is not required for an owner’s statutory liability under Civil Code § 3342. Other claims and potential defendants require a separate analysis.

Can I bring a claim if I fell but was not bitten?

Possibly. The dog bite statute specifically concerns bites; a fall or other non-bite injury may support a negligence or other claim depending on the evidence.

Ask about deadlines promptly

California’s general deadline for an injury or wrongful-death lawsuit is two years under Code of Civil Procedure § 335.1. Different rules and exceptions can apply, including to medical negligence and claims involving children. Claims against public entities generally require an earlier claim presentation, often within six months under Government Code § 911.2. Do not assume that negotiations with an insurer extend a deadline. A consultation should address the particular dates and defendants in your case.
