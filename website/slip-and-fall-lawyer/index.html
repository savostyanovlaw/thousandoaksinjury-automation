Premises Liability Evidence

The condition that caused the fall can disappear quickly.

Preserve the condition

Photographs, video, measurements, surveillance footage, and witness accounts may help establish what existed when the incident occurred.

Investigate notice

Inspection practices, prior complaints, maintenance records, and how long a condition existed can become important depending on the case.

Document the injury

Medical records, imaging, treatment recommendations, lost income, and the effect on daily activities help establish claimed damages.

Common Conditions

Premises cases are fact-specific.

Claims may involve wet or slippery floors, uneven walking surfaces, broken pavement, defective stairs or handrails, poor lighting, debris, or other dangerous conditions. A fall alone does not establish liability; the evidence must connect the condition and responsible party to the injury.

Slip and Fall FAQ

What evidence matters after a slip and fall?

Photographs or video, incident reports, witness information, medical documentation, and evidence concerning inspection or notice can become important.

Does a fall automatically make the property owner responsible?

No. Responsibility depends on the condition, control of the property, notice, causation, comparative fault, and other facts.
